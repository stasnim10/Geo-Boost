I need you to make the "Show me on AI" web app usable for non-technical small business owners — think a local plumber, restaurant owner, or consultant with zero SEO/web-dev background. Right now the interface uses technical language that this audience won't understand. Please audit the app and make the following changes.

CONTEXT
The product runs an "AI Visibility Audit" that scores a business 0–100 on how well AI assistants (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity) can find and recommend them. The score is a composite of four technical checks currently labeled: "Citation Rate," "Semantic Density," "Structural Formatting," and "Technical Access" (which covers Bing indexing, robots.txt, and schema markup). Users then get a paid Fix/Monitor/Grow plan to address issues.

PROBLEM
The scoring dimensions, technical checks, and recommended fixes are written in SEO/developer language. A non-technical user can see they scored "34/100" but won't understand why, what "robots.txt" or "schema markup" means, or what to actually do about it. This creates confusion and drop-off instead of urgency to buy.

WHAT TO FIX

1. Replace jargon with plain language everywhere it appears (score labels, tooltips, email reports, action items). Examples of the translation needed:
   - "Citation Rate" → "How often AI mentions you by name"
   - "Semantic Density" → "How clear and specific your website content is"
   - "Structural Formatting" → "How easy your site is for AI to read"
   - "Technical Access" → "Whether AI robots can even see your website"
   - "robots.txt is blocking GPTBot" → "A hidden setting on your website is telling AI 'do not look at this business' — this is likely costing you customers"
   - "Schema markup missing" → "Your website isn't telling Google/AI basic facts about your business (hours, location, services) in a format they understand"
   - "Bing indexing" → "Whether your business shows up in the index ChatGPT uses to find businesses"
   Keep the technical term available in a small "for my web developer" expandable section so power users and developers aren't stripped of precision — but the default view should never assume technical knowledge.

2. Add a plain-language layer to every score and result. Every number, badge, or technical flag should have a one-sentence explanation next to it in everyday words, not just a tooltip that requires a hover/click to discover it exists.

3. Simplify the results page hierarchy. Don't show all four technical dimensions with equal weight up front. Lead with: the overall score in plain terms ("Critical — AI is not recommending you"), the single biggest issue in one sentence, and one clear next action. Progressive disclosure: let users expand into "show me the details" if they want more.

4. Make every recommended fix actionable without technical skill. Instead of describing the problem, give a copy-paste snippet or a "forward this to your web developer" button that packages the technical instruction, so the business owner never has to understand robots.txt themselves — they just need to know something is wrong and how to get it fixed.

5. Add a short, friendly onboarding (2–3 screens max) before the first audit runs, explaining in plain terms what the tool does and why it matters — no assumption the user knows what "AI visibility" or "GEO" means. Use a simple analogy (e.g., "Imagine a new employee who only reads certain files — if you don't give them the right information, they can't help your customers find you").

6. Add a persistent "What does this mean?" pattern — small (?) icons next to any term that isn't everyday English, opening a one-sentence plain explanation, not a technical glossary entry.

7. Rewrite all email reports (weekly Monitor emails, audit results) in the same plain-language style. Assume the reader is scanning on a phone in under 30 seconds.

8. Keep the visual design, brand colors, and existing layout structure — this is a language and information-hierarchy fix, not a redesign. Don't change pricing, plans, or backend logic.

ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
- A person with no SEO or web development background should be able to read any screen and understand what their score means and what to do next, without clicking anything.
- No unexplained technical term should appear in the default (non-expanded) view anywhere in the product, including emails.
- Every fix/recommendation should end in a concrete, non-technical action ("send this to your web developer," "click here to fix automatically," etc.) rather than a technical description alone.

Please start by showing me the current copy on the results page and audit report so we can agree on the plain-language replacements before you implement them across the app.
